AI infrastructure

An AI service is a coupled system of compute, networking, storage, orchestration, serving, and observability. Accelerators do the model math; the surrounding layers determine whether that capacity becomes reliable user-visible work.

Observability

Metrics, traces, logs, evaluations, and cost. Join resource pressure to task outcomes.

Optimizing one layer can move the bottleneck elsewhere. A faster GPU does not help when storage starves it, placement fails, or queue policy violates the latency budget.

02

Two Different Data Flows

Training

Object store→CPU workers→Accelerators→Checkpoints

Throughput and utilization dominate; collective communication and checkpoint I/O are system-wide constraints.

Online inference

Gateway→Scheduler→Model server→Response

Queue time, prefill, decode, and cache policy combine into user-visible latency.

03

Agentic Workloads Shift the Balance

WorkloadDominant patternResource emphasis
TrainingLarge parallel batchesAccelerators and fabric
Batch inferenceHigh-throughput generationAccelerators and serving runtime
AgentsSequential reasoning and toolsCPU, RAM, network, storage, accelerators

Shell commands, browsers, parsers, compilers, retrieval, and verification are CPU-native. Multiple worktrees and processes can exhaust RAM or file descriptors before model cost becomes the limiting factor.

ServiceRequest rate, errors, queue time, TTFT, inter-token latency

Distributed traces should join model calls, retrieval, tools, and verification into one request path. Dashboards show load; traces explain a particular slow or failed task.

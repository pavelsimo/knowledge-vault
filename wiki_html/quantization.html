Model compression

Quantization, measured

Quantization maps high-precision weights into smaller numeric formats. The gain is lower memory traffic and footprint; the cost is approximation error, metadata, and runtime-specific trade-offs.

01

Precision at a Glance

16-bitBF16 / FP16

2 bytes per weight; common baseline for training and serving.

8-bitINT8

1 byte per weight; often near-baseline quality with compatible kernels.

4-bitINT4

0.5 byte per weight before scales and metadata; common for local inference.

Methods and Failure Modes

MethodIdeaWatch for
Block quantizationGive small groups separate scalesMetadata versus outlier containment
GPTQUse second-order information during PTQCalibration data and backend support
AWQProtect weights important to large activationsKernel and checkpoint compatibility
SmoothQuantMove activation difficulty into weightsWeight/activation scaling balance
QLoRATrain adapters over a frozen 4-bit baseFine-tuning method, not a universal serving format

Below 4 bits, error can rise sharply. Quality must be evaluated on the intended tasks; file size alone is not a sufficient acceptance criterion.

Research Trail

  • GPTQ — accurate post-training quantization for generative transformers.
  • AWQ — activation-aware weight quantization.
  • SmoothQuant — weight-and-activation INT8 quantization.
  • QLoRA — adapter fine-tuning over a quantized base model.
